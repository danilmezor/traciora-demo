## The story

1. The web chat is outside opening hours. The assistant answers from public knowledge pages (what a grinding noise can mean, what an inspection costs, opening hours). Rule R13: her first message is unclear ("it makes a noise, mostly in the morning"); the assistant asks once whether it is when braking or when turning, and she says braking. The assistant cannot book: it raises a scheduling hand-over card (kind `task`, normal priority, unowned) and tells her when the shop opens (7:30; ru 9:00) and that someone will get back to her as soon as possible: the product's hand-off sentence names no time.
2. Slot 6 takes the card, opens an appointment (source web chat) and rings her. The call is outbound: it has a transcript and no score.
3. The inspection finds worn front pads and rotors and a seeping rear shock absorber. The estimate reaches her by e-mail from the shop system (10:05; ru 10:40). She approves the brakes by message and declines the shock absorbers (the right rear one is seeping), which are marked deferred and raise a reminder. She pays by card (15:10; ru 18:40).
4. She comes back to the website chat from the same phone. The web chat remembers a visitor for thirty days, so she is the same contact, a "Website visitor": her name and number are in the first chat's hand-off note and on its card, not on the contact. The assistant answers from the public price list, cannot book and hands over; slot 6's reply at 16:05 makes the chat hers, and from that line the assistant only drafts.
5. In a live demo this is the chat in which the junior edits the waiting draft and sends it: a reply is sent in the web chat only, and the other channels are history.
