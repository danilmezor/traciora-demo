# Parts pricing matrix

*Claire Brennan with Marcus Gaines · Last reviewed {{@price_rise|date}}*

Parts are priced from what we pay, by gross margin on the selling price. List price = cost ÷ (1 − margin).

| Kind | Margin |
|---|---:|
| Parts, economy tier | 60 % |
| Parts, standard tier | 56 % |
| Parts, premium tier | 48 % |
| Fluids | 58 % |
| Batteries | 40 % |
| Tires | 22 % |

Worked on the common front pad set: economy costs us $24.00 and sells at $60.00; standard $38.00 and $86.36; premium $52.00 and $100.00. The cheap part carries the highest margin and the dear part the lowest, so the customer who buys better parts is not punished for it.

Sublet work is priced job by job. A transmission rebuild costs us $2,600.00 and is billed at $3,400.00.

- Marcus applies the matrix when a part is added to the catalogue. Advisors do not reprice parts.
- When a supplier's cost moves, the list price moves with it at the next catalogue update, not with the labour rate. That is why parts did not change on {{@price_rise|date}} and labour did: part prices are the ones we went live with on {{@golive|date}}.
- Company-rate accounts get 10 % off the list price, which leaves us about half the margin on a standard part. That is the price of their volume.
- The whole business should come out near 51 % on parts. If a month is under 48 %, look for hand-typed parts and for economy sold where standard was quoted.
